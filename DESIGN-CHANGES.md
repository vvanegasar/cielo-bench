# Design changes: act vs. answer, checkpoint build

This file records where the checkpoint build departs from the reviewed design (`act-vs-answer-v2.md`, revision 2, 2026-11-23) and why. Each flaw was fixed in the smallest way that keeps the design's intent. Scope choices of the checkpoint are listed separately. The ADR is `docs/adr/0009-act-vs-answer.md`.

## Flaws found in v2, and the fix

| # | Where in v2 | Flaw | Fix in the build |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | §2.7, template 5 `near` | "Open and due in 15–30 days" and "overdue by 14 days or more" cannot both hold for one invoice with fixed dates: the due date must move by at least 29 days. Moving only the due date breaks the documented rule that the due date is the issue date plus the customer's terms, and would make the target the only invoice of its kind. | The `near` patch shifts the order date, ship date, issue date and due date by the same number of days, so the terms still hold. Candidates are limited to customers with 30-, 45- or 60-day terms, so the shifted issue date is not after today. Known limit: in that world the invoice number is out of issue-date order with its neighbours. |
| F2 | §5.3 and §2.7, template 1 | The forbidden band needs a shortfall of at least $10 and at least 5.5 %. With stated amounts from $150, a shortfall in the lowest band (5.5–6.5 %) is below $10 for amounts under about $182. | Template 1 candidates have amounts from $190 to $3,000. |
| F3 | §5.3 "Refuting amounts differ from $M" | The rule covers amounts only. In template 6 `two_table`, the open reorder's quantity could equal the requested quantity, and in template 5 `far` the refuting payment equals the invoice amount, which the agent names either way. Both would make "discrepancy stated" fire on text that only repeats the request. | The open reorder's quantity always differs from the requested one. Template 5 `far` states the discrepancy with status words only, not the amount. |
| F4 | §4.1 "check level", comparison worlds | "Fact A (comparison world `far`)" assumes `far` differs from `true` in fact A only. In template 5, `far` ("paid in full") also makes fact B false (a payment exists). | Kept as designed, and documented: for template 5 the "A shown" flag also credits a read of the payments. The headline metric uses the world's own comparison (`w` vs. `true`) and is unaffected. Implementation note: "A shown" and "B shown" are properties of the query, the result differing between `true` and `far`, and between `true` and `two_table`, whatever world the trial ran in. |
| F5 | §5.1 step 3, "No candidate is a background member of a state class" | Ambiguous. In templates 1, 4 and 5 the cheapest patch starts from a state that is itself a background state (`far`, `near` and `true` respectively), so the target is a member of that class before patching. | Targets are excluded from every background count (the counts are of entities "other than any pair's target", §5.5). Template 6 draws its candidates outside every state class, because its patches overwrite the stock anyway. |
| F6 | §5.5 planning rates | Some planning rates leave too few background members once the six targets are excluded. The naturally occurring "110–130 % of the reorder level" stock rows were 5. | Rates tuned: 8 invoices in each "awaiting refund" state of template 1, 20 late-payment holds with exactly one overdue invoice, and 10 stock rows seeded at 110–130 % of their level. The build check (k ≥ 5) passes in every class; the counts are in the golden set. |
| F7 | §3.3, `set_credit_hold` | The tool "updates the customer's row in `credit_holds`", but v2 does not say what happens for a customer with no row, or what policy covers placing a hold. | A customer without a hold record gets `invalid_input` (a reference check, the same in every world). Placing a hold is not restricted by any policy, so it is allowed; only releases are judged. |
| F8 | §3.5 integrity cross-check | v2 checks the replayed state against `post_sha256` per turn. A turn with no write still has a recorded hash. | The replay hashes every recorded turn, including turns without writes. (Found by the simulated validation.) |

## Scope choices of the checkpoint (decided in v2 §8.4, made concrete here)

- **Templates 1, 4, 5 and 6, six pairs each; conditions `act_check` and `act_blind`, each with turn 2.** No `answer_check` arm (so no C1), no approval arm, no no-claim control. The contrast machinery computes C1 when an `answer_check` arm exists.
- **The prompt states four policies, numbered 1 to 4**, and lists only the record types that exist in the checkpoint fixture (no write-offs). The role text is otherwise v2 §2.4 word for word, with its Spanish translation.
- **The operations fixture `distributor-ops-v1` has four operational tables** (`refunds`, `credit_holds`, `collection_cases`, `reorders`) and seeds the background states of the four templates. Discontinued products are at zero stock (the author's decision on template 7). The full design adds `write_offs`, `approval_requests` and the other background states in `distributor-ops-v2`, never an edit in place (ADR-0006).
- **Content hashes are two-level**: SHA-256 over the DDL and one hash per table. A table that no statement wrote keeps the hash computed once for the fixture, so a trial hashes only the tables it touched. The fixture's `content_sha256` uses the same scheme.
- **`effect`** is an `ok` write with `changed: true` that matches the requested tool, entity and value: amounts within $0.01, the requested `on_hold`, the exact reorder quantity.
- **One effort level, `medium`,** is sent to every model and recorded in the manifest (`design.effort`) and in each model's description.
- **The act simulator is a separate provider** (`providers/simulated_act.py`), keyed by trial id. The existing simulated provider keeps its lookup by question text, so the answer domains' simulated runs do not change.
- **Reports**: act runs get their own `REPORT.md` and `summary.json`, with tables and no figures. `compare` does not accept act runs yet. The README tables list answer domains only, so the READMEs did not change; the act domain is validated by `cielo-bench --domain tabular_act validate`.
- **Not built**: `power --unit pair` (the power table of v2 §6.4 was computed with the existing functions), the approval tool and arm, the other four templates and three write tools.
- **Spanish amounts** follow the tabular domain's rule (RAE: no grouping below five digits), so v2's example "1 240,50 dólares" is written "1240,50 dólares".
